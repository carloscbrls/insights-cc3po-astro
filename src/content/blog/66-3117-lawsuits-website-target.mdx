Federal ADA Title III website accessibility lawsuits hit **3,117 in 2025** — a 27% jump from the year before. That is not a typo. Over 3,000 businesses, most with fewer than 50 employees, were served with federal lawsuits because their websites were not accessible.

## The Numbers Do Not Lie

Here is what the data shows:

- **3,117 federal ADA website lawsuits** filed in 2025
- **27% increase** year-over-year
- **73% of defendants** had fewer than 50 employees
- **California alone:** 478 lawsuits per month, driven by the Unruh Civil Rights Act ($4,000 per violation)
- **Illinois surged 745%** as plaintiff firms relocated from New York
- **Shopify stores** represented 32% of all web accessibility lawsuits

## Who Is Being Targeted?

This is not just happening to big corporations. **Small businesses are the #1 target.** Plumbers, dentists, restaurants, HVAC companies, retail shops — any business with a website that the public can visit is exposed.

In California, the Unruh Civil Rights Act adds a statutory minimum of **$4,000 per violation** on top of federal ADA claims. A single lawsuit can cost $10,000–$50,000+ in legal fees and settlement costs.

## The "Accessibility Widget" Trap

Here is a hard truth: **26% of businesses that were sued had an accessibility widget installed.** Overlay widgets like AccessiBe, UserWay, and AudioEye do not make your site compliant. Courts have repeatedly ruled that widgets are not a substitute for real WCAG compliance.

In fact, the National Federation of the Blind and over 400 accessibility advocates have signed a statement saying overlay widgets should not be used. Installing one can actually *increase* your legal risk by signaling you knew about accessibility issues but chose a band-aid instead of a real fix.

## What Actually Protects You

Real compliance means:

- WCAG 2.2 AA conformance — the standard courts measure against
- Proper alt text on all images
- Keyboard navigation that works
- Sufficient color contrast ratios
- Missing legal pages: Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Accessibility Statement, DMCA Notice
- Regular audits — compliance is not one-and-done

## The 4 Pages Most Businesses Are Missing

Across 100% of the businesses we scanned, the same four pages were missing:

1. **Privacy Policy** — Required by CCPA and expected in ADA lawsuits
2. **Terms of Service** — Establishes your legal boundaries
3. **Accessibility Statement** — Shows good-faith effort toward compliance
4. **DMCA Notice** — Required if anyone can upload content to your site
