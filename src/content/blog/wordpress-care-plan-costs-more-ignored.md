## What Actually Breaks When You Ignore Your WordPress Site

Let me be specific, because vague warnings don't help anyone.

### 1. Plugin Conflicts

WordPress runs on plugins. The average small business site has 15-25 active plugins. Every time one of those updates, there's a chance it breaks something else. I've seen a single WooCommerce update take down an entire product page. I've seen a security plugin update lock the admin panel. When nobody's watching, these conflicts sit there for weeks — sometimes months — before anyone notices.

### 2. Core WordPress Updates

WordPress pushes major updates 3-4 times a year. Minor updates happen constantly. If you're not applying them, you're running software with known security holes. That's not a hypothetical risk — it's an open door with a sign that says "come on in."

### 3. Broken Forms and Broken Revenue

This one hits the bottom line directly. I audited a dental practice's site last month where the appointment request form had been returning errors for 3 weeks. Three weeks of patients thinking they booked appointments that never went through. That's real revenue walking out the door, and nobody noticed because nobody was checking.

## The Real Cost of "Free" Maintenance

Here's what I hear all the time: *"My nephew handles the website."* Or, *"I just log in and click update when I remember."*

That's not maintenance. That's hope dressed up as a strategy.

Real WordPress maintenance means:

- **Weekly**: Plugin updates tested on a staging environment, not live on your production site
- **Weekly**: Backup verification — not just "backups are running" but actually restoring one to confirm it works
- **Monthly**: Security scanning, performance benchmarking, broken link checks
- **Monthly**: Database optimization and spam cleanup
- **Quarterly**: Full content audit, accessibility check, SEO health review

How much of that is your nephew doing?

The honest answer for most Central Valley businesses: none of it. And it shows. I've run free audits on local business sites where I found 40+ accessibility violations, 12 outdated plugins, and contact forms that had been silently broken for months. These are businesses paying $200/month for hosting and getting zero maintenance included.

## What a WordPress Care Plan Actually Covers

I build care plans because I got tired of emergency calls. Here's what I include — and what you should expect from any legitimate plan:

- **Updates with staging**: Every plugin, theme, and core update gets tested before it goes live. Not on your live site. On a copy. That's non-negotiable.
- **Verified daily backups**: Stored off-site. Tested monthly. If your backup hasn't been restored in a test environment, it's not a backup — it's a prayer.
- **Security monitoring**: Real-time scans, not "I'll check next week." Brute force protection, malware scanning, and uptime monitoring that alerts me before you even know there's a problem.
- **Performance tuning**: Core Web Vitals tracking, image optimization, database cleanup. Your site should load in under 2 seconds. If it doesn't, we fix it.
- **Accessibility checks**: WCAG 2.2 AA compliance monitoring. Not because lawsuits are scary (they are) — because it's the right thing to do and it expands your audience.

## 3 Things You Can Check Today (Before You Even Call Me)

I'm not here to sell you something you don't need. Here are three things you can check right now, for free:

1. **Check your backup**: Log into your hosting panel. Find the backup section. Check the date on your last backup. If it's older than 7 days, you have a problem. If you can't find the backup section, you have a bigger problem.

2. **Run a speed test**: Go to PageSpeed Insights and test your site. If your mobile score is under 50, your site is losing visitors. Period. 40% of people leave a site that takes more than 3 seconds to load.

3. **Test your forms**: Fill out your own contact form. Submit it. Check if you actually receive the email. Do this on your phone too. You'd be surprised how many forms break on mobile.

If any of those three things failed, you need a care plan. Not next month. Now.
