On November 18, Google added a new model to the Gemini 4 family: Neon. If you already use the Gemini app, or you pay for the Gemini API to build something, this is the one in the middle. Google says it is built for work that needs serious reasoning, but should finish faster and cost less than the company's top-tier model.

That is Google's claim, not an independent test. This post sticks to what Google published, what it costs, who can use it, and the jobs Google itself says Neon is for — the kind of messy, multi-document work that eats an afternoon in a small business.

## What Google launched

Koray Kavukcuoglu, Google's chief AI architect, <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">introduced Gemini 4 Neon</a> on November 18, 2026. The official description is "frontier-level intelligence with fast end-to-end task completion across complex software engineering, knowledge work, and science workflows."

In the Gemini 4 lineup, Google puts three models in a simple order. Carbon is for the hardest, long-horizon projects. Hydrogen is for fast, everyday work. Neon, Google says, "balances depth and efficiency" and "delivers frontier-level reasoning for complex, high-frequency workflows faster and more cost efficiently."

The model can take spreadsheets, PDFs, charts, audio, and video in a single context window of 1 million tokens, and turn that material into structured deliverables, according to the same announcement. A 1-million-token window is large enough, in practical terms, to hold a long report, a stack of invoices, or a mixed folder of files in one conversation — though the useful limit still depends on how much you attach, and on your plan's usage caps.

The <a href="https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-4-neon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">DeepMind model card</a>, also published November 18, repeats that positioning. It also notes a limitation worth keeping in mind: Neon's knowledge cutoff is March 2026, and in some domains the model may be less current. Google says the model can still pull in details from the web for fast-moving topics. Like any model, it can also get things wrong. Check the numbers, the citations, and anything you would not want to send to a customer unreviewed.

## Who can use it

Availability is narrower than "anyone with a Gemini window."

In the <a href="https://gemini.google/subscriptions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Gemini app</a>, Google says Pro and Ultra subscribers can select Gemini 4 Neon from the model drop-down. Neon is listed on Google AI Pro and on Google AI Ultra. It is not listed on the free plan or on Google AI Plus, which Google lists with Gemini 4 Hydrogen.

Paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can also use Neon in <a href="https://antigravity.google/blog/gemini-4-neon-in-google-antigravity/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Antigravity</a>, Google's agentic development tool, and in Google AI Studio.

Developers can call it on the <a href="https://aistudio.google.com/docs/whats-new-gemini-4-neon" target="_blank" rel="noopener">paid tier of the Gemini API</a> in Google AI Studio. The model ID is <code>gemini-4-neon</code>. It is not available on the free API tier.

For companies, Google says Neon is rolling out in Gemini for business, through the API or the model drop-down. Plan details and seat prices vary; see Google's <a href="https://gemini.google/business/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Gemini for business</a> page for the current options.

## What it costs

For a person in the United States, Google's subscriptions page currently lists Google AI Pro at $19.99 a month and Google AI Ultra starting at $99.99 a month, with a higher Ultra tier at $199.99 a month for more usage. Those are the US list prices as shown on November 26, 2026. Prices and limits can differ by country, and usage limits in the Gemini app depend on the complexity of what you ask, not just a simple message count.

If you are calling the API, Google's published price for Neon is $3.00 per million input tokens and $15.00 per million output tokens. Cached input tokens are $0.15 per million, which Google describes as a 95% discount; the developer docs say this is implicit caching, and that explicit caches are not supported. Output pricing includes thinking tokens. The API path is paid-tier only.

Google also says that, at Neon's default "high" thinking level, the model has "a 32% lower cost per task than Carbon." That figure comes from Google, on its own announcement. We have not independently measured cost per task.

Developers can also pick one of four thinking levels — low, medium, high (the default), and xhigh — to trade depth, speed, and token spend. Most small-business readers using the Gemini app will not need to think about that setting. If you are on the API, start with the default and only move up when the default is not enough.

## Where it can actually save time

The useful part of the announcement, for a shop that is not training models, is the list of jobs Google itself gives Neon in the Gemini app.

Google says Neon is stronger at writing and knowledge work: it reads uploads such as spreadsheets, PDFs, and photos more precisely, turns dense material into reports and summaries, and can pull relevant details from Google apps like Gmail and Docs.

It also highlights research — for example, comparing prices across vendors before a major purchase, or pulling together a brief with current market data.

And it says Tasks in Gemini can now run on Neon, for multi-step chores that usually take a pile of tabs. The examples Google gives are ordinary: analyzing billing statements to see why a utility charge spiked, or cross-checking receipts and documents to negotiate a refund.

Those are Google's examples, not a promise that the model will catch every error or win every refund. Used carefully, they are a reasonable place to start: one real stack of documents, one question you already know how to check, and a few minutes to compare the answer with the source files.

If you write software, Google also positions Neon for multi-file debugging and refactoring, and for long agent workflows in Antigravity. That is a different lane. The same rule applies: try it on a contained task, and review the result.

## The point

Gemini 4 Neon is Google's mid-tier Gemini 4 model, announced and rolling out on November 18. In the Gemini app, it is a Pro and Ultra feature. On the API, it is paid-tier only, at published token prices, and Google says it costs less per task than Carbon.

If you already pay for Pro, the practical move is simple. Pick Neon from the model drop-down the next time you have a messy, multi-document job — a vendor comparison, a long PDF, a folder of receipts — and see whether it actually saves you time. Then check the work.
