# The DOJ Extended the ADA Deadline — But Your Lawsuit Risk Didn't Pause

If you heard the Department of Justice extended its ADA compliance deadline and thought *"great, I've got more time"* — you need to read this carefully.

The extension applies to **public entities** under Title II. It does **not** shield private businesses from the surge of ADA Title III lawsuits that are breaking records right now.

Here's what actually changed, what didn't, and what you should do today.

---

## What the DOJ Extension Actually Covers

In early 2026, the DOJ pushed the compliance deadline for **ADA Title II** — which applies to state and local government entities — to **April 2027**. This gives public agencies more time to bring their websites and digital services into WCAG 2.1 AA compliance.

That sounds like a big deal. And for government websites, it is.

But here's the critical distinction most small business owners miss:

**Title II = government entities. Title III = your business.**

The DOJ extension does not apply to private businesses. Restaurants, retail shops, HVAC companies, plumbers, dental offices — if you serve the public, you're covered under Title III, and that deadline didn't budge.

## The Numbers That Should Get Your Attention

The 2025 ADA lawsuit data tells a story that no extension can pause:

- **8,667 federal ADA Title III lawsuits** were filed in 2025 — a 27% increase over 2024
- **California leads with 3,252 lawsuits** — more than any other state by a wide margin
- **New York follows with 2,100+ cases**
- **Florida and Texas round out the top four**

These aren't hypothetical risks. These are filed, active federal cases. And the trend is accelerating — not slowing down.

### Why the Surge?

Three forces are driving the explosion:

1. **AI-powered demand letter generation.** Tools now scan thousands of websites automatically, identify WCAG violations, and generate demand letters at scale. What used to require manual review now happens in bulk.

2. **Pro se filings jumped 40%.** Plaintiffs are representing themselves more often, which means lower barriers to filing and higher volume. ChatGPT and other AI tools have made it trivially easy to generate legal filings without attorney fees.

3. **Repeat plaintiffs.** A small number of serial filers account for a disproportionate share of cases. Some individuals have filed hundreds of ADA lawsuits, often targeting small businesses that are least likely to fight back.

## The Conduent Case: Developers Can Be Sued Too

Here's a wake-up call that flew under most people's radar.

In the **Conduent case**, a web developer was held liable for **$2 million** in damages for building an inaccessible website. The reasoning: the developer knew (or should have known) that the site they built needed to be accessible.

This isn't just a theoretical risk anymore. If you're a developer, freelancer, or agency building websites for clients, **you can be named in an ADA lawsuit** for the sites you create.

For small business owners: this means the "my web guy said it was fine" defense doesn't hold up. Both the business and the builder can be held responsible.

## What Actually Changed vs. What Didn't

Let's be crystal clear:

### What Changed
- **Title II compliance deadline** extended to April 2027 for government entities
- Government agencies have more time to remediate their websites and digital services

### What Did NOT Change
- **Title III requirements** — still in full effect for private businesses
- **Private lawsuit activity** — increased 27% year over year
- **State-level requirements** — California's Unruh Civil Rights Act remains one of the strongest in the nation
- **Damages** — up to $4,000 per violation under California law, in addition to federal damages
- **Developer liability** — confirmed via the Conduent ruling

If you're a private business, nothing about your compliance timeline changed. You're still exposed today.

## What to Do Right Now

Don't wait for a demand letter. Here's your action plan:

### 1. Audit Your Website Immediately

Run a WCAG 2.1 AA compliance scan. You need to know exactly where your vulnerabilities are. Use our [free ADA compliance scanner](https://cc3po.com/audit) — it takes 60 seconds and requires no signup.

### 2. Fix High-Impact Issues First

Not all violations carry equal risk. Prioritize:

- **Alt text missing on images** — the #1 most cited violation
- **Keyboard navigation failures** — forms and menus that require a mouse
- **Color contrast problems** — text that's hard to read against backgrounds
- **Missing form labels** — contact forms without proper labels for screen readers
- **Skip navigation links** — missing "skip to content" links

### 3. Install an Accessibility Overlay for Immediate Protection

An overlay like Accessibly or UserWay gives you immediate, visible compliance effort. It's not a complete fix — no overlay is — but it shows good faith and provides functional improvements while you work on deeper remediation.

### 4. Document Everything

If you get sued, the court wants to see that you've taken compliance seriously. Keep records of:

- When you ran your audit
- What issues you found
- Your remediation timeline
- What fixes you've completed

Good faith effort matters in settlement negotiations and court proceedings.

### 5. Set Quarterly Re-Scan Reminders

Compliance isn't one-and-done. Every time you update your site — new blog post, new product, new image — you introduce potential violations. Quarterly scans catch problems before plaintiffs do.
