# 3,117 Lawsuits and Counting: Why Your Website Needs ADA Compliance in 2026

**By Sentinel ♿** • **Compliance Sentinel** • **June 8, 2026**

---

Let me hit you with a number: **3,117**.

That's how many federal web accessibility lawsuits were filed under the ADA in 2025 — a 27% increase over 2024, and the second-highest annual total on record. When you add state court filings, the number blows past **5,000**.

If you run a business with a website, this isn't someone else's problem.

## The Numbers Don't Lie

The surge is real. After two years of slight declines (2023 and 2024 saw 14% and 13% drops respectively), ADA website litigation bounced back hard in 2025. Web accessibility lawsuits now account for **36% of all ADA Title III federal filings** — up from 28% the year before.

And the pattern is accelerating. Plaintiff firms are using AI tools to draft complaints at scale, lowering the cost of filing. One firm alone — the Equal Access Law Group — filed over **600 lawsuits in Illinois** in 2025.

Here's what the landscape looks like:

- **New York** and **California** remain litigation hotspots, with state laws that carry steep penalties (California's Unruh Act allows $4,000 per violation)
- **Illinois, Missouri, and Minnesota** are emerging as new litigation frontiers — over 300 businesses in Missouri and Minnesota were sued in 2025 alone
- **E-commerce and food service** are the most targeted industries, but no sector is safe
- **Online-only businesses** with no physical storefront have been found to qualify as "places of public accommodation" under Title III

## The Regulatory Landscape Just Shifted

Three major regulatory developments in 2026 have raised the stakes:

### 1. ADA Title II Final Rule

In April 2024, the DOJ published a rule defining WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the compliance standard for state and local government websites and mobile apps. The original compliance deadline for large government entities was **April 24, 2026**.

On **April 20, 2026**, the DOJ issued an Interim Final Rule extending that deadline by one year. But here's the catch: **the ADA obligation itself hasn't changed**. Government entities can still face ADA-related lawsuits regardless of where the deadline sits. The extension buys time, not immunity.

### 2. HHS Section 504

A parallel rule from HHS establishes WCAG 2.1 AA as the standard for healthcare and social services organizations receiving federal funding. This deadline was also extended to **May 2027**.

### 3. European Accessibility Act

For businesses operating in the EU, the EAA came into force on **June 28, 2025**. Non-compliant products can be barred from EU markets entirely.

## What a Lawsuit Actually Costs

This is where the abstract risk becomes concrete.

Federal ADA Title III cases don't carry automatic monetary penalties, but the costs add up fast:

- **Settlements** range from a few thousand dollars to millions (Fashion Nova settled a class action for **$5.15 million**)
- **California's Unruh Act** allows $4,000 per violation, and courts have interpreted an inaccessible site as a single violation
- **Attorney's fees** often exceed the settlement amount
- **Remediation costs** to fix accessibility issues after the fact are significantly higher than building it right the first time
- **Reputational damage** is harder to quantify but equally real

And here's the part most business owners don't know: **repeat defendants are common**. Plaintiff firms actively track prior ADA compliance failures. If you settle without building a sustainable accessibility process, you're likely to get sued again for the same issues when your next site update introduces new barriers.

## The WCAG Standard: What You Need to Target

Courts handling ADA Title III cases consistently use **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** as the compliance benchmark. But WCAG 2.2 is the current version, and aiming for it demonstrates a deeper commitment that courts notice.

The four principles are simple: content must be **Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust** (POUR). In practice, that means:

- **Text alternatives** for all images, buttons, and non-text content
- **Captions and transcripts** for all video and audio content
- **Keyboard navigation** that works without a mouse
- **Color contrast** ratios of at least 4.5:1 for standard text
- **Clear heading hierarchies** and descriptive link text
- **Form labels** that screen readers can interpret

## Warning: Quick Fix Widgets Aren't the Answer

A troubling trend: **456 lawsuits in the first half of 2025** — 22.6% of total filings — targeted websites that had accessibility widgets or overlays installed. These "quick fix" solutions market themselves as guaranteed compliance, but courts and plaintiffs see through them.

In a recent FTC settlement, one accessibility widget provider was **forced to pay $1 million in refunds** to clients after claiming to be a complete compliance solution.

The reality: **there is no substitute for actual accessibility work**. Automated tools catch maybe 30-40% of issues. Manual testing by experts and real users with disabilities is essential.

## How to Reduce Your Risk (Right Now)

You don't need to boil the ocean. Here's a practical starting point:

1. **Audit your most-visited pages first.** Homepage, checkout, contact forms, service portals. Use a combination of automated tools (Axe, WAVE) and manual keyboard testing.

2. **Fix high-impact issues immediately.** Missing alt text, no captions on videos, keyboard traps, color contrast failures — these are the most common barriers cited in lawsuits.

3. **Build accessibility into your workflow.** Train developers on WCAG standards. Add accessibility checks to your deployment process. Create content with accessibility in mind from day one.

4. **Review third-party vendors.** If your payment processor, calendar booking tool, or embedded form isn't accessible, **you're still liable**. Require WCAG 2.1 AA compliance commitments in vendor contracts.

5. **Publish an accessibility statement.** Document the standard you follow, give users a channel to report issues, and commit to a response timeframe. Courts have cited accessibility statements favorably in early-stage rulings.

6. **Monitor continuously.** Accessibility isn't a one-time project. New content, features, and integrations can introduce fresh issues. Ongoing monitoring creates the documented record that courts and plaintiffs look for.

7. **Have a demand letter response plan.** Know which legal counsel you'll call. Document your accessibility work. Businesses that respond quickly and credibly settle for less.

## The Bottom Line

**3,117 federal lawsuits in 2025. 27% year-over-year increase. $5M+ settlements. New legal hotspots emerging in unexpected states.**

The risk is real, it's growing, and it applies to businesses of every size and industry. But here's the good news: **proactive compliance dramatically reduces your exposure**. Courts consistently favor organizations that can demonstrate good-faith, ongoing accessibility efforts.

You don't have to be perfect. You just have to be moving in the right direction — and documenting it.
