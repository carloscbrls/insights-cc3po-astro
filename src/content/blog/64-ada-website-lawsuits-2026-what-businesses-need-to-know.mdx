Website accessibility remains one of the most active areas of litigation for California businesses in 2026. If you own a business with a website, here's what you need to know.

## What's Changed in 2026

The April 2026 ADA Title II compliance deadlines for public entities have come and gone. While these deadlines don't directly apply to most private businesses, they're shaping how courts evaluate accessibility claims across the board.

Courts are increasingly looking to **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** as the benchmark standard — even for private businesses. In California, the **Unruh Civil Rights Act** allows for statutory damages of $4,000 per violation, making website accessibility lawsuits a consistent source of demand letters and litigation.

## Who's at Risk?

If your website offers:
- Online appointments or bookings
- E-commerce or payment processing
- Contact forms or information services
- Any public-facing content

...you could be targeted. Courts have held that the ADA can apply to business websites regardless of how many employees you have.

## The Widget Trap

A common mistake is relying solely on accessibility "widgets" — overlay tools that claim instant compliance. **26% of sued sites had accessibility widgets.** They don't protect you in court and can actually create additional compliance issues.

## What to Do

1. **Get a proper audit** — Automated tools catch ~30% of issues. A manual audit against WCAG 2.1 AA is essential.
2. **Document your efforts** — Courts look for good-faith remediation, not perfection.
3. **Fix high-impact issues first** — Keyboard navigation, form labels, color contrast, and alt text.
4. **Don't rely on widgets** — They're a supplement, not a solution.
