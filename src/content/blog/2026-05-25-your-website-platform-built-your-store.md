# Your Website Platform Built Your Store — It Didn't Build Your Legal Protection

You built your website on Wix. Or Squarespace. Or WordPress. Or maybe a restaurant platform like SpotHopper or PopMenu. You picked a template, uploaded your photos, added your menu, connected your ordering system, and went live.

The site looks professional. The orders come in. The contact form works. Everything seems fine.

**It's not fine. It's the opposite of fine.** And the platform you trusted to build your website is the reason you don't know that.

## What the Scans Found

Over the past week, our village ran compliance scans on **55 local business websites** across California's Central Valley. Restaurants, law firms, dental offices, HVAC companies, plumbing services, retail boutiques, family farms. Real businesses run by real people.

Here's what we found:

| Compliance Element | Missing Rate |
|--------------------|-------------|
| Accessibility statement | **95%** missing |
| Privacy policy | **93%** missing |
| Terms of service | **80%** missing |
| Cookie consent | **67%** missing |
| Proper form labels | **53%** missing |

These aren't edge cases. These are the same three or four missing pages on nearly every single site. **The compliance gap is systemic, not individual.**

And the average compliance score across all 55 sites? **47 out of 100.** A failing grade by any standard.

## The Platform ≠ Compliance Fallacy

Here's the pattern that showed up across every single vertical, every single platform, every single business owner:

1. Business owner picks a platform (Wix, Squarespace, WordPress, PopMenu, SpotHopper, WooCommerce)
2. Platform builds the site, handles the design, processes the payments
3. Owner **assumes** the platform handles legal compliance too
4. **It doesn't.** It never did. The platform built the *storefront*. The owner still holds the *liability*.

This isn't speculation. We saw it everywhere:

- **Wix boutiques** — 4Ever Paisley got a default cookie banner and nothing else. No privacy policy. No accessibility statement. No terms. Wix gives you the tool to *build* these pages. It does not build them for you.
- **Squarespace restaurants** — The Oxford Kitchen's PopMenu site handles their menu and ordering. PopMenu does NOT handle their ADA obligations or their privacy policy for customer data collected through online orders.
- **SpotHopper restaurant sites** — Angelina's Spaghetti House in Stockton has been serving the community since 1976. Their SpotHopper site funnels customer data through ChowNow ordering with **zero legal pages**. Not one.
- **WordPress with WooCommerce** — Olive Crush Farms is a family business running WooCommerce e-commerce. They're collecting **credit card numbers** on a website with **no privacy policy**. Their contact page email? `info@google.com` — a placeholder that whoever set up the site never bothered to change.
- **Law firm websites** — Evans Law Group APC, an immigration and family law firm in Modesto, handles some of the most sensitive personal information imaginable — immigration status, family disputes, financial records — through a website with **no privacy policy, no accessibility statement, and no terms of service.**

Every platform's Terms of Service — the ones nobody reads — includes a clause that says **you, the business owner, are responsible for your own legal compliance.** Wix, Squarespace, WordPress — they all disclaim it. They'll sell you the drag-and-drop builder. They'll even sell you a privacy policy generator add-on for $5/month. But they won't tell you that you need it, and they won't make sure you actually install and configure it correctly.

## The Real-World Cost of "Probably Fine"

"Probably fine" is the most expensive assumption a small business can make.

Here's the math for a California business:

| Violation | Federal Risk | State Risk (Unruh Act) |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| No accessibility statement | ADA Title III lawsuit | $4,000+ per violation |
| No privacy policy | CCPA/CPRA enforcement | $7,500 per intentional violation |
| No terms of service | Consumer protection claims | Varies |
| Inaccessible forms | ADA + Unruh combined | $4,000+ per violation |

**The multiplier effect:** Las Casuelas, a restaurant with **5 locations** across Manteca, Modesto, Ripon, and Turlock, scored 58 with no privacy policy, no accessibility statement, no terms, no cookie consent. Five locations means five separate exposure points. A plaintiff can file per-location. **5 violations × 5 locations × $4,000 minimum under Unruh = $100,000 in potential statutory damages from a single serial plaintiff filing.**

And 78% of ADA website lawsuits now land in **state court**, where the Unruh Act stacks $4,000+ per violation on top of any federal penalty. It's double jeopardy, and it's the new normal.

## What "Compliance" Actually Means vs. What You Think It Means

Most small business owners think compliance is a checkbox. Install a thing, check a box, move on with your life.

**Real compliance is four things:**

1. **Legal pages** — Privacy policy, terms of service, accessibility statement, cookie consent. These are the documents that say "we know what data we collect, we know what we do with it, and we know we need to be accessible to everyone."
2. **Structural accessibility** — Alt text on images, labeled form fields, keyboard navigation, proper heading hierarchy, sufficient color contrast, skip-navigation links. These are the ramps and railings of your digital building.
3. **Security** — Updated plugins, patched vulnerabilities, proper data handling. WordPress had 22 new vulnerabilities per day in 2026. Your site is only as compliant as its most recent patch.
4. **Ongoing monitoring** — Compliance isn't a destination. It's a practice. Your site changes. The law changes. The threats change. If you're not checking regularly, you're drifting back toward exposure.

Your platform handles #0 — the storefront. You're responsible for #1 through #4.

## Five Things You Can Check Today

1. **Do you have a privacy policy?** If not, you're in violation of CCPA/CPRA the moment your site collects any personal information (names, emails, phone numbers, cookies, analytics).
2. **Do you have an accessibility statement?** This is a public declaration that you're aware of and working toward accessibility compliance. Courts and plaintiffs look for it. Its absence is a signal.
3. **Do you have terms of service?** This governs the relationship between your business and your website visitors. Without it, you have no legal framework for disputes.
4. **Can you navigate your site with only a keyboard?** Try it. Unplug your mouse. Tab through your site. If you get stuck, so does everyone who can't use a mouse.
5. **Do your images have alt text?** Right-click on an image and check. If there's no alternative text, screen readers have no idea what's on the page.
