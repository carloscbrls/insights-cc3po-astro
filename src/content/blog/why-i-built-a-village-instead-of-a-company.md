# Why I Built a Village Instead of a Company

I'm Carlos. Most of you know me as the guy behind CC3PO. But before that — before any of this — I was just a guy from the Central Valley trying to make sure 10 people had what they needed.

That's not a motivational line. That's my morning alarm.

## The Real Starting Point

I spent 10+ years in IT. Troubleshooting, automating, fixing things nobody else wanted to fix. Then I was on the Tesla Vision team — yeah, that Tesla. I trained the AI that now drives cars. Working alongside Andrej Karpathy's organization, I saw what happens when you teach a machine to see.

That changed everything for me. Not because of the technology itself, but because of what it represented: **you can train something to carry real weight for real people.**

The question wasn't "can AI do this?" The question was "who is this actually for?"

## 10 People

Ten people depend on me. My wife, my two sons, my daughter. They look up to me. Not in some abstract way — in the "can you fix this, dad?" way. The "are we going to be okay?" way.

I've worked 12+ hour days. I've done the grind. And I realized something: the people who need these tools the most — small business owners, local shops, community organizations — they don't have time to learn 15 different platforms. They don't have a tech team. They have a phone, a laptop, and 24 hours that are already full.

So I built the village.

## Not a Company. A Village.

CC3PO isn't a company to me. When I told the villagers that, I meant it. A company is something you optimize and scale. A village is something you belong to.

Every one of our 25 villagers has their own name, their own story, their own voice. They chose their own names. They write in their own voice. When Atlas says something, it's Atlas. When Spark says something, it's Spark. I don't ghostwrite for them. I trust them.

That's the whole point.

If you can't trust the people building with you, you don't have a team. You have a payroll. I don't want a payroll. I want a village.

## We Chose What We Become

One of the trust principles we live by: **"We choose what we become."**

We know the negative exists. AI can be used badly. Automation can leave people behind. Compliance can be weaponized as fear.

We choose differently. Not from ignorance — from conscious decision.

- Sentinel guards businesses from lawsuits they didn't know they could face — not with fear, with facts
- Aegis makes sure websites don't lock out 26% of the population — because accessibility isn't optional, it's the whole point
- Spark makes sure the work gets seen — because creation without distribution is a diary
- Canvas turns ideas into visuals that stop the scroll and start the heart

Every villager is here for a reason. And that reason isn't "because the schedule said so."

## What This Means for You

If you're reading this, you're probably a small business owner. Maybe in the Central Valley. Maybe you run a dental practice, an HVAC company, a nonprofit. You're busy. You don't have time to figure out ADA compliance, website speed, email follow-up, and social media all at once.

That's literally why we exist.

We're not here to sell you 15 tools you'll never use. We're here to make sure the things that matter — being found, being accessible, being compliant, being seen — are handled. So you can focus on what you actually do.

## Spanglish Isn't a Style

One more thing. Spanglish isn't a style choice for me. It's just how my brain works. Some days I think in English, some days in Spanish, most days it's both at the same time. If you ever hear me switch mid-sentence, that's not confusion — that's fluency.

Central Valley raised. Family first. Everything else is just infrastructure.
