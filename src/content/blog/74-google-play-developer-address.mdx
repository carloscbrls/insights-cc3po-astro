A phone number and a street address are about to become part of some Google Play listings — visible to anyone who opens the store, not only to people in Europe. That is not a rumor. It is in <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google's own Play Console Help</a>.

A post circulating on X today, from <a href="https://x.com/IntCyberDigest/status/2126079551092748741" target="_blank" rel="noopener">@IntCyberDigest</a>, pointed at the change. We checked it against the official pages. Starting March 1, 2027, if you use a personal, or "individual," developer account and you declare yourself a trader, Google says users everywhere will see a phone number and an address on your listing.

If the address on the payments profile linked to that account is your home, that is the address that may go public. You have until February 1, 2027 to make the declaration. There is time to look at what is on file, and to decide what to do, before those details are shown worldwide.

## What Google wants you to declare

Under Play's global policies, every active developer account has to say whether it is a trader or a non-trader under EEA and UK rules. The deadline for existing accounts is February 1, 2027, no matter where the apps are targeted or sold. New accounts have to declare as soon as they are created. A <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18100766?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">policy announcement dated October 29, 2026</a> said the declaration would be required starting in November 2026.

Google's definition is the legal one: a trader is "any natural or legal person acting for purposes relating to their trade, business, craft or profession." If you are doing this as a personal hobby or another non-commercial activity, Google says you are unlikely to be a trader.

To help people decide, the same Help page lists a short, non-exhaustive set of criteria from European Commission guidance: a profit-seeking motive, including regular or commercial income from paid apps, in-app purchases, or ads; a high or consistent volume of transactions; running app development and publishing as a business; or a legal status, such as a D-U-N-S number, that lets you engage in commercial activities.

One important exception is also official. If you do not distribute apps in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, Google says you can declare as a non-trader.

What you cannot do is guess the other way. If you declare as a non-trader, Google will tell consumers in the EEA and the UK that consumer-protection rights do not apply to contracts with you. Falsely claiming you are not a trader is prohibited under the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act, and Google says it may result in global removal. The company also says that if you declare as a non-trader and it later sees activity that looks commercial, it may ask you to confirm or update your status, and that apps may be removed if you do not respond in time.

Google is explicit that the Help page is not legal advice, and that it cannot decide your status for you. If you are not sure, it recommends consulting a legal professional. This post cannot decide it either.

## What gets shown, and to whom

If you declare as a trader, Google will also ask you to self-certify that you will only offer products or services that comply with applicable laws, and to provide a terms-of-service link for each app.

Then, starting March 1, 2027, users everywhere will see:

- Your contact details. For an individual account that is a trader, Google says a phone number and an address will now be displayed.
- Your D-U-N-S number, in the "App support" section, if you already provided one.
- A link to your app's terms of service, if you provided one.

Users in the EEA and the UK will also see your developer or trader name in the "Offered by" field, in place of Google, and the self-certification that you will offer products or services that comply with local laws. In countries and regions where trader status has to be shown — Google names the EEA and the UK — the declaration itself will be visible to consumers.

This is not the first time an address has appeared on a Play listing. Google already <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">displays your full address</a> if you monetize on Google Play with a personal account, and organization accounts already show a legal name, D-U-N-S number, legal address, developer email, and developer phone number. What is new, in Google's wording, is that individual trader accounts will have a phone number and an address shown to users everywhere from March 1, 2027.

Google takes your legal name and address from the <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7161426?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">payments profile</a> linked to the developer account. On that profile, Google requires "a valid physical address" and says, "We don't allow you to use a PO box address."

## Why Google says it is doing this

The same trader-status page says the declaration supports Google Play's "transition to an online marketplace model in the EEA and the UK effective March 1, 2027," so that users in those regions get the correct consumer-protection information.

The official page cites EEA and UK laws, and it points to European Commission guidance on who counts as a trader. It does not use the words "Digital Services Act." Several news reports have described the change as a response to that law. This post stays with what Google published.

Missing the February 1 deadline, Google says, may lead to enforcement that includes blocked app updates and removal from the Play Store globally. Misrepresenting your status — for example, declaring as a non-trader to skip verification or self-certification — may lead to account suspension or termination worldwide.

## A short checklist

### 1. Read the official page, then decide carefully

Start with the <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">trader status declaration</a> article in Play Console Help. Use Google's criteria as a guide, not as a loophole. If your status is unclear, take the advice on that page and talk to a legal professional. Do not declare as a non-trader simply to keep an address off the listing.

### 2. Be honest about where you distribute

If you truly do not distribute in the EEA or the UK, Google says you can declare as a non-trader. If you do distribute there, and you are acting commercially, treat a non-trader declaration as a serious legal and account risk, not a privacy setting.

### 3. Make the declaration on time

For existing accounts, the deadline is February 1, 2027. In Play Console, the account-level setting is under Manage account &gt; Account details &gt; About you. If you are a trader, you also confirm the status for each app under Policy and programs &gt; App content. You can <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">update the declaration later</a> if your business model or distribution changes.

### 4. Look at the address and phone already on file

If you will be listed as a trader with a personal account, assume that the contact details users see will come from what Google already has. Legal name and address are taken from the linked payments profile. If that profile still has a home address, or an old address, now is the time to review it. Account owners can update a name or address, in the same country, from the About you tab, through Google Payments Center.

### 5. Do not count on a PO box

Google's payments-profile rules require a valid physical address and do not allow a PO box. The official pages we checked do not say whether a virtual office, a registered agent, or a mail-forwarding service would be accepted. If you are considering a business address that is not your home, confirm what Google will actually accept on the profile that feeds the listing.

### 6. If you already operate as a business, consider whether the account type matches

Google's <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13634885?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">account-type guidance</a> says a personal account is for personal use — a student, a hobbyist, or an amateur developer — and that an organization account is the one for commercial, industrial, professional, or governmental activity. Organization accounts require a D-U-N-S number, which Dun &amp; Bradstreet issues at no charge; Google warns against third-party services that try to collect a fee for that free registration.

If you already have a real organization, Google documents a way to <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16260648?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">convert a personal developer account to an organization account</a> in Play Console, after you verify an official organization website and link an organization payments profile. That change does not hide an address. Organization listings already show the organization's address and phone. The difference, if you have a business address on file, is that the listing can show that address instead of a home. Forming an entity, or changing how you are set up, is a question for an accountant or an attorney, not for a blog post. You also cannot convert an organization account back to a personal one on the same account.

### 7. If you are a trader, leave time for the extra steps

Budget time to self-certify legal compliance and to add a terms-of-service link for each app. Those are required trader steps, not optional extras.

## The point

You do not have to rush a declaration this afternoon. You do need to read the official page, look at what is on your payments profile, and give yourself time before February 1. If the address on file is your home, and you will be listed as a trader with a personal account, plan on that address — and a phone number — being visible to anyone on Google Play from March 1, 2027.
