# ADA Compliance in 2026: What Every Small Business Needs to Know

If you run an HVAC company, a plumbing shop, or a local retail store, this isn't a "read later" situation. It's a "read right now" situation.

ADA website lawsuits have exploded over 300% since 2018. The average settlement hits **$4,000 per violation** — and that's per page element, not per website. A single site with 10 inaccessible elements could face $40,000 in damages. These aren't scare tactics. These are filed cases in federal court.

The worst part? Most small business owners don't know they're vulnerable until a demand letter shows up in the mail.

## Why Small Businesses Are the Target

Here's what most people get wrong: they think ADA lawsuits only happen to big corporations. That was true in 2015. It's not true anymore.

A growing industry of "accessibility testers" systematically scans small business websites, finds WCAG violations, and sends demand letters offering to settle for $3,000–$10,000. These aren't lawsuits filed by disabled users who couldn't use your site. They're compliance drives — automated scans looking for low-hanging fruit.

Why small businesses? Because:

- **Small sites are rarely compliant.** Most were built with basic WordPress themes or Wix templates that don't follow WCAG standards.
- **Owners settle quickly.** $4,000 is cheaper than hiring a lawyer.
- **Nobody checks until it's too late.** Compliance isn't on anyone's to-do list until the letter arrives.

HVAC companies, plumbers, dentists, restaurants, and local retailers are the top targets. If your site has a "Book Now" button that a screen reader can't identify, that's a violation. If your images don't have alt text, that's a violation. If your color contrast is too low, that's a violation.

## What "Compliant" Actually Means

ADA compliance for websites means following **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** guidelines. That's the standard courts reference. Here are the big ones:

1. **Alt text on all images.** Every image needs a description that screen readers can read aloud.
2. **Keyboard navigation.** Every feature must work without a mouse — using only Tab, Enter, and arrow keys.
3. **Color contrast.** Text must have at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio against its background.
4. **Form labels.** Every form field needs a visible label, not just placeholder text that disappears.
5. **Consistent navigation.** Menus and links should work the same way on every page.
6. **No auto-playing media.** Videos and audio shouldn't start without user action.

That's not the full list — there are 50+ success criteria — but those six account for the majority of violations.
